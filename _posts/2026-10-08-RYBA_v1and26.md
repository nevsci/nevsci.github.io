**RYBA brings molecular editing, ORCA input generation, output analysis and scientific reporting into one local workspace.** You can prepare a molecule, set up its calculation, inspect the results and turn them into figures or Supporting Information within the same program.

The idea came from teaching computational chemistry to synthetic chemists. Finding an up-to-date input generator with enough options was a recurring hurdle: we needed more than an optimization and a short list of functionals. I started building an interface with broader choices and explanations of the settings. That grew into **RYBA — Refine Your Bonds and Atoms**, now at version 1.26.

## Prepare the molecule and the calculation

Start with XYZ coordinates, reuse a geometry from an earlier calculation, or build a molecule in the **Geometry Workshop**. Reference rings and functional groups provide starting points; atoms, substituents, bond orders, distances, angles and dihedrals can all be edited. Local MMFF94/MMFF94s refinement helps prepare starting structures, including selected regions with fixed atoms.

The **Input Builder** covers geometry optimization, frequencies, electronic energies, excited states, GOAT conformer searches, scans, transition-state searches, NEB and IRC. Jobs can be chained—for example, optimization → frequencies → TD-DFT—and saved as reusable templates.

For experienced users, the method catalogue includes DFT, double hybrids, composite methods and selected post-Hartree–Fock approaches, and can be extended. Basis sets, dispersion, solvation, approximations and numerical settings remain explicit. Compatibility checks help catch avoidable input errors before export.

## Understand what the calculation produced

The **Results Analyzer** extracts structures, energies, frequencies and excited-state data from ORCA outputs. You can inspect convergence, select geometries along an optimization, measure internal coordinates and animate vibrational modes. Extracted values retain source information for checking the original output.

Calculated electronic transitions can be displayed as **UV–Vis spectra**, with adjustable broadening and overlays for comparing molecules or methods.

The **GOAT Explorer** examines conformers: different three-dimensional arrangements of the same molecule. Linked plots, tables and molecular views let you filter by energy or population, compare heavy-atom and all-atom RMSD, and overlay two structures. Aligning a rigid core makes differences in flexible side chains much easier to see. Ranked energies, populations and energy-versus-coordinate plots provide complementary views of the ensemble.

The **Thermochemistry workspace** combines calculations into energy diagrams with molecular structures. Choose electronic energies, ZPE-corrected energies, enthalpies or Gibbs free energies. ORCA-printed and corrected Gibbs values are distinguished, including Grimme qRRHO and Cramer–Truhlar options. Compatibility checks flag inconsistent calculation protocols and thermochemical conventions before comparison.

## Turn the results into something useful

RYBA exports molecular figures, spectra and thermochemical diagrams, along with the underlying data. Its **Supporting Information generator** creates editable Word documents with coordinate tables, selected properties, excited-state or GOAT summaries, and general methodology profiles. Geometry, electronic-energy and thermal sources can be assigned separately for multi-level workflows.

For presentations, a separate **PowerPoint export** emphasizes structures, concise methodology and selected plots.

The standalone application runs as a single HTML file in your browser, without installation, an account or uploading molecular data. Prepare the input in RYBA, run it in ORCA, then load the output back into RYBA for analysis.
